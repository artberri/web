- I agree with most of Félix López's [El fin de la profesión, o no](https://flopezluis.substack.com/p/el-fin-de-la-profesion-o-no).
- Other parts of the business haven't adapted that well to the new AI workflows.
- The bottleneck isn't development anymore. Fewer people can do the same work, so it looks like some people are left over ("sobran").
- But the problem might be that other roles still want to work the same way as before, and keeping the same output flow needs fewer developers.
- Maybe those other roles should adapt how they work too, and delegate some of their work to developers, especially the ones who have always been product developers and not just coders.
- Shahid N. Shah and Andy Oram, [When AI Can Code, What Is Left for Programmers to Do?](https://www.oreilly.com/radar/when-ai-can-code-what-is-left-for-programmers-to-do/) (16 November 2026): developers shouldn't hand-write specs, they should talk with the product manager and the customer and let AI turn those conversations into specs. Close to the idea that other roles hand part of their work to product developers. Also: "With AI coding, reviewing has suddenly become much more expensive than writing."
